In the story of Adam and Eve, depicted here in stained glass, paradise is lost. By listening to a serpent instead of God’s clear orders, they fall into sin, which requires them to leave God’s presence in the garden of Eden to live difficult lives in the fallen world.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/Hemera Technologies

God commanded Abram, later called Abraham, to use his son Isaac as a sacrifice to Him, a scene shown in this 1880s lithograph. Abraham listened faithfully and prepared the sacrifice; however, God provided a ram for Abraham to sacrifice instead. God had wanted to test Abraham’s commitment to his faith, not to kill his only remaining son.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/duncan1890

This ancient page shows the famous tale of the parting of the Red Sea. Pursued by hard-hearted Egyptians, the Israelites are trapped by the sea until Moses stretches out his hands over the water. The water parts, so the nation of Israel walks between the walls of water to safety on the other side of the sea. The Egyptians pursue, but the army perishes under the wall of water.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/Photos.com

One of the possible locations for Mount Sinai is shown here. God descended on Mount Sinai and provided Israel with the Ten Commandments, clarifying how those who love God should approach Him and live in the world.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/Photographer, artist

Samson’s elderly parents obeyed God and placed him under special vows, which included that he should not let a razor cut his hair. He consistently breaks most of the vow and sins greatly. Delilah, the Philistine woman shown in this engraving by French artist Gustave Doré (1832–1883), deceives him and cuts his hair. Samson loses his strength and eyes, but God returns Samson’s strength one more time to kill many Philistines.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/ivan-96

This painting shows a scene in which Job discusses his plight to his friends. God has taken away everything from Job and his wife (his ten children’s lives, his clothes, and his physical health) to prove to Satan that Job will be loyal to God despite these tests. Even when Job’s life is destroyed, he does indeed stay faithful to God and receives all that he had before and more as a reward for his faith.

Photo credit: © Ilya Repin, Public domain, via Wikimedia Commons

The New Testament Gospels of Matthew, Mark, Luke, and John all focus on the works of Jesus, God’s son. Only two of the Gospels tell of the birth of baby Jesus, and the many guests present after the Virgin Mary delivered him. This chromolithograph published in 1886 is one of thousands of artistic renditions of this momentous event.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/ZU_09

This nineteenth-century chromolithograph shows Jesus preparing to turn water into wine at a wedding in Galilee—one of the miracles described in John 2. John and other authors of the Bible share stories of these miracles to prove that Jesus is the Son of God.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/ZU_09

This vintage engraving celebrates two women in the Bible: Naomi and Ruth. After their husbands have died, Ruth leaves her extended family to go with her mother-in-law, Naomi, to Israel to care for her, and to trust Israel’s God. God cares for the women through an Israelite, Boaz, who obeys the Torah and marries Ruth.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/duncan1890

Formerly named Saul, Paul the Apostle was a very important missionary. He went to Turkey, Greece, Spain, Jerusalem, and Rome to preach the gospel of Jesus Christ. He is meant to serve as a prime example of a devout Christian, and is celebrated in many works of art, such as this marble relief.

In this nineteenth-century fresco of the Last Supper, painted by Leopold Kupelwieser in the nave of a Vienna church, Jesus eats a traditional Passover meal with his disciples. Jesus presents himself as the slain lamb who dies so that sinners might live.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/sedmak

The famous portrayal of Jesus on the cross—shown here in a nineteenth-century chromolithograph—is symbolic of the faith of Christians worldwide. Though the Son of God was tortured and killed, Jesus died for the sins of his people. Three days after this tragic scene, he rose from the dead.

Photo credit: © Getty Images/ZU_09

Jerusalem, shown here in modern times, is one of the most important cities in the New and Old Testaments. Not only did Jewish people use Jerusalem as the capital of their nation; it is also said to be a place for God’s Kingdom when He returns.
